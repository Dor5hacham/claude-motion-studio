01How a video gets made

Claude does not paint pixels by hand and does not use an AI video generator. It writes a program or a scene file. A renderer runs that program once per frame. FFmpeg then joins the frames and the sound into a video file.

1 · YOUDescribe the result: length, look, techniques, timing.
→
2 · CLAUDEWrites code (shaders, Canvas, React, Python) or builds a scene in Blender.
→
3 · RENDERERDraws every frame. 60 seconds at 60 fps is 3,600 pictures.
→
4 · FFMPEGJoins the frames and the audio into an MP4.

Frames and frame rate

A video is a fast sequence of still frames (top). The number of frames per second (fps) changes how smooth the same motion looks (bottom).

Render at 60 fps for smooth UI motion; use 24 fps if you want a film look.
Frame. One still picture.
fps. Frames per second: 24 film, 30 web, 60 smooth.
Resolution. 1920×1080 landscape, 1080×1920 vertical, 1080×1080 square, 3840×2160 4K.
Render. The computer drawing the frames. Render time is how long that takes.
Real-time vs offline. Real-time draws fast enough to watch live (games, websites). Offline takes as long as it needs per frame and saves a file.
GPU vs CPU. The GPU draws millions of pixels or particles at once. The CPU is better at step-by-step work like physics or sound.
Timeline. What happens at which second. Every video prompt should include one.
Encode. Compressing frames into a video file. H.264 MP4 plays almost everywhere.
Delivery formats. MP4 (H.264) for everything; WebM or ProRes 4444 when you need transparency (alpha) to overlay; GIF for chat; Lottie JSON for apps. FFmpeg makes all of them.

02Motion vocabulary

These words describe how things move. Use them in prompts and Claude knows exactly what you mean. Each one is live below.

Easing

How speed changes during a move. Nothing real starts or stops at full speed, so motion without easing ("linear") looks robotic. A steep part of the curve means fast, flat means slow.

Easing race

All nine easings start and end at the same moment. Watch who leads early, who overshoots, and who bounces.

Stagger

The same move on many items, each starting a little later. The order is the pattern.

Stagger the tiles from the center out, 40 ms apart, each with an ease-out-back pop.

Squash and stretch

Flatten on impact, stretch when moving fast. Makes things feel soft and alive.

Bouncing ball with squash and stretch, flatten 40% on contact.

Anticipation and overshoot

A small move back before the main move, and a move past the target before settling.

The card pulls back slightly, then shoots right and overshoots before settling.

03With vs without

Each pair shows the same animation twice. Left: without the idea. Right: what changes when you put those words in your prompt.

Timing

How long a move takes. Short feels snappy, long feels calm or heavy.

Snappy timing: 250 ms moves.

Hold

A pause where nothing moves, so people can read. Text needs about 1 second per 4 to 5 words. The bar shows in, hold and out.

Hold the title for 1.5 s before it exits.

Follow-through and overlap

Loose parts keep moving after the main body stops, and settle at different times.

Add follow-through to the ribbons when the logo stops.

Secondary motion

Small extra moves that support the main action: dust on landing, a wobble after impact.

Add a small dust puff and a wobble when it lands.

Arcs

Natural motion follows curves. The dots are positions at equal time steps.

Move the icon along an arc, not a straight line.

Rhythm and beat sync

At 120 BPM a beat is 0.5 s. Left: changes land between beats (coral ticks). Right: every change lands on a beat. Turn on the click track to feel the difference.

120 BPM, every cut and pop lands on the beat.

Seamless loop

The last frame flows into the first. Left jumps back at the loop point; right never shows the seam.

Make it a seamless 2-second loop.

Parallax

Near layers move faster than far layers. Left: all layers move together and the scene looks flat. Right: depth.

Three-layer parallax as the camera slides right.

06Post effects

Effects applied to the finished picture, like filters. Switch them on and off on a frame from the reel, and use the split to compare before and after.

Bloom. Bright parts glow.
Film grain. Fine noise; hides banding, adds texture.
Vignette. Darker corners pull the eye to the center.
Chromatic aberration. Red and blue split at the edges, like a cheap lens.
Color grade. The overall color look, often done with a LUT.
Depth of field. Only one distance is sharp.
Motion blur. Fast objects smear, like a real shutter.
Letterbox. Black bars for a wide cinema frame.

08Text in motion

Typography that moves. Usually Canvas 2D, CSS or Remotion, and cheap to render.

092D motion graphics

Shapes, lines, paths, depth layers and isometric scenes: the After Effects style of explainer videos and app promos.

10App and web UI motion

Small, fast animations inside interfaces, made with CSS, SVG and JavaScript, or libraries like Framer Motion and GSAP. These go into your app as code, not as video.

11Simulation and generative art

Motion from rules instead of keyframes: physics, flocking, growth, particles, noise and math.

12GPU shaders

A shader is a small program that runs on the graphics card for every pixel or point, every frame. A vertex shader places points; a fragment shader colors pixels. GLSL is their language and WebGL2 is how a browser reaches the GPU.

13Sound and motion

Motion driven by audio. For a video, Claude analyzes your track the same way and syncs cuts and effects to it, or synthesizes music in Python as it did for the reel (kick, bass, pads and risers from sine waves and noise, with hits on every cut).

14Animation libraries

The same kinds of motion made with popular open-source libraries. Name the library in your prompt when you want code you can keep using in your own project.

153D engine (Blender)

What a browser cannot simulate well: cloth, liquid, fire, shattering, photoreal light. Each clip was built by a Python script and rendered headless in Blender 5.2 on this PC. The scripts are in source/blender. Two renderers: Eevee is fast (about half a second per frame here) and Cycles traces real light paths for glass, caustics and soft bounce light (several seconds per frame). Ask for Cycles when you want photoreal. Unreal Engine 5 is also installed; Claude drives it through its MCP server while the editor is open.

16Code-to-video tools

Tools that turn code into finished video files: React (Remotion), HTML (HyperFrames), Python (Manim), TypeScript (Motion Canvas), and Three.js or WebGPU scenes rendered frame by frame. Sources are in source/tools.

17Editing and post-production tricks

What Claude can do to footage you already have, with FFmpeg and Python. Scripts are in source/edit.

19How to prompt for motion

A good motion prompt reads like a director's brief. Hover a part on the left to see it in the example prompt.

Deliverable. Length, resolution, fps, format, where to save.
Purpose. Who watches and why.
Look. Mood, palette (hex codes), fonts, references.
Technique. Names from this page.
Timeline. What happens at which second. The most useful part.
Motion feel. Easing, stagger, timing.
Camera. Static, orbit, push-in, depth of field.
Text on screen. Exact words and when they appear.
Sound. None, synthesized, or your track with beat-synced cuts.
Checks. Ask for test frames before the full render.
Make a 20-second 1920x1080 60 fps MP4 on my desktop as the opener for my product launch talk. Look: dark #0b0b10 background, coral #ff5a36 and cyan #2bc4e6 accents, Bahnschrift type, premium and calm. Techniques: kinetic typography, then GPU particles in WebGL2 that form the logo. 0-4 s the title rises through a mask; 4-12 s particles swirl and form the logo; 12-20 s logo holds, tagline types on, fade out. Sound: synthesize a calm pad with a hit when the logo forms. Show me 6 test frames as a contact sheet before the full render.

Mood to motion, live

The same title animated six ways. Each mood is a recipe of timing, easing, stagger, palette, camera and finish.

Habits that save time

Ask for test frames

"Render 6 test frames as a contact sheet first." You see problems before a long render.

Give seconds

"At 3 s the logo lands" beats "then the logo lands".

Point at a card

"Like the fluid simulation card, but in my brand colors." Every card here has a name and a source file.

Change one thing at a time

"Keep everything, but make the transitions faster." Small edits are cheap.

Name the tool when it matters

"In Blender" for realistic physics, "in WebGL" for millions of particles, "in Remotion" for template videos.

Say what to avoid

"No lens flares, no rainbow colors, no text smaller than 28 px."

23Glossary

Additive blending. Overlapping colors add up and glow. see it
Alpha. Transparency. A video "with alpha" can sit on top of other footage.
Ambient occlusion. Soft darkening in corners and cracks. see it
Bake. Calculate a simulation once and store it, so rendering is repeatable. see it
Bezier curve. A smooth curve set by handles. Easing curves are Bezier curves. see it
Bloom. Glow around bright areas. see it
Boids. Flocking agents that follow three simple rules. see it
BPM. Beats per minute. Sets the rhythm for cuts. see it
Compositing. Combining layers into the final image.
Contact sheet. Many frames in one grid image, to check a video at a glance.
Curl noise. Swirling flow without clumping; the standard for particle flow. see it
Datamosh. Deleting keyframes so motion smears pixels. see it
Depth of field. Only one distance is sharp. see it
Domain warping. Feeding noise coordinates through more noise; marble and liquid looks. see it
Dolly zoom. Camera moves back while zooming in; the background stretches. see it
Easing. How speed changes during a move. see it
Eevee / Cycles. Blender's fast renderer / Blender's realistic path tracer. see it
fBm. Noise layered at several scales for natural detail. see it
Flow field. A grid of directions that particles follow. see it
Follow-through. Parts keep moving after the body stops. see it
FLIP fluid. A liquid simulation that moves particles and turns them into a surface. see it
Compute shader. A GPU program that does general math, like moving a million particles. see it
Fragment shader. GPU program that colors each pixel. see it
Geometry nodes. Blender's node system for procedural geometry. see it
GLSL / WGSL. Shader languages of WebGL and WebGPU.
Grease Pencil. Blender's tool for hand-drawn 2D lines inside a 3D scene. see it
H.264. The most common video codec.
Headless. A program running with no window, controlled by code.
Instancing. Many copies of one object in a single GPU call. see it
Isometric. A 3D look with no perspective: parallel lines stay parallel. see it
Keyframe. A value set at a specific time; software fills in between.
Kinetic typography. Animated text. see it
LUT. A lookup table that remaps every color; used for grades. see it
Lottie. A JSON animation format for apps and websites. see it
Mask / matte. A shape that hides or reveals part of a layer. see it
Metaballs. Blobs that merge smoothly. see it
Motion blur. Streaking of fast objects. see it
Noise. Smooth randomness; the base of most organic effects. see it
Optical flow. Estimating motion between frames to invent new in-between frames. see it
Parallax. Near layers move faster than far layers. see it
Procedural. Made by rules and math, not by hand.
Ray marching. Stepping a ray through a distance field to find surfaces. see it
Reaction-diffusion. Two simulated chemicals that grow organic patterns. see it
Rigid body. A physics object that does not bend. see it
SDF. Signed distance field: a formula for the distance to the nearest surface. see it
Speed ramp. Smoothly changing playback speed inside one shot. see it
Spring. Physics-based easing set by stiffness and damping. see it
Stagger. The same move on many items, offset in time. see it
Stable fluids. A real-time fluid solver. see it
Tone mapping. Turning very bright render values into screen colors (AgX, ACES).
Trim paths. Animating how much of a line is drawn. see it
Truchet tiles. Simple tiles that form endless patterns when rotated. see it
Vertex shader. GPU program that places points and corners. see it
Vignette. Darkened edges of the frame. see it
Volumetrics. Rendering smoke, fog and fire as light passing through a volume. see it
Voronoi. Cells around random points. see it
